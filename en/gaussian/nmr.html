NMR: from shielding to chemical shift

Gaussian's NMR keyword (GIAO method) gives the shielding constant σ (ppm) of each nucleus. The chemical shift δ is obtained from σ in one of two ways.

  • Reference difference: δ = σ_ref − σ, with σ_ref of TMS computed under the same conditions.
  • Calibration equation: δ = (b − σ)/m from a regression over many molecules. The coefficients depend on nucleus, functional, basis set and solvent (e.g. CHESHIRE).

For flexible molecules, compute σ for each conformer, average by population, then convert to δ. This assumes conformer exchange is fast on the NMR time scale.

Watch the direction of the calibration equation

The same m and b mean different things depending on whether the paper writes δ = (b − σ)/m or σ = b + mδ. For example, the SI of Bally & Rablen (2011) gives δ = (31.844 − σ)/1.0205 for ¹H at WP04/cc-pVDZ in chloroform. CHESHIRE tables use σ = mδ + b with a negative slope m (here −1.0205). These coefficients apply to this level only.

Record σ, the averaged value and the converted δ separately, and compare residuals/MAE against experiment for each candidate structure.

Procedure

  1. Prepare the conformer set

    Build a conformer set with populations following Conformers and stable structures.

  2. Run NMR on each conformer

    Pick one of the conditions below and use it for every conformer.

  3. Average σ over conformers and convert to δ

    Use a reference difference or a calibration equation.

  4. Compare each candidate with experiment

    Compare MAE/RMSE per nucleus. For DP4+, choose the statistical parameters that match the calculation level.

mPW1PW91/6-31+G(d,p), PCM(CHCl₃)

One of the most common combinations for natural products, with B3LYP/6-31G(d) geometries. Also the standard level for DP4+.

PBE0/6-311+G(2d,p), PCM(CHCl₃)

Geometries at B3LYP/6-31+G(d,p). PBE0 is written PBE1PBE in Gaussian. The papers cited use ¹³C δ = (187.3123 − σ)/1.0533 and ¹H δ = (31.7532 − σ)/1.0958 (CHESHIRE values, determined with SMD(chloroform) shieldings).

From shielding σ to chemical shift δ

  • Direction: if the paper writes σ = b + mδ, then δ = (σ − b)/m.
  • Average over conformers at the shielding stage, then convert (with a linear calibration the order does not matter).
  • OH and NH protons are usually excluded because exchange and hydrogen bonding dominate.

The error from a regression fitted to the same compound's own data is not evidence that the structure is correct. Compare candidates (e.g. DP4+) or use coefficients determined on other molecules, such as CHESHIRE's.

A protocol with dedicated corrections: Hehre et al. 2019

For ¹³C of flexible natural products: conformer screening in Spartan, ωB97X-D/6-31G(d) shieldings and empirical corrections depending on atom environment and bond lengths. Mean rms error below 4 ppm over about 925 compounds (for the full protocol).

Step Level Screening
1. Conformer search MMFF within 40 kJ/mol, duplicates removed
2. Optimization HF/3-21G within 40 kJ/mol
3. Single point ωB97X-D/6-31G(d)//HF/3-21G within 15 kJ/mol
4. Reoptimization ωB97X-D/6-31G(d) within 10 kJ/mol
5. Populations ωB97X-V/6-311+G(2df,2p)[6-311G(d)] Boltzmann weights from dual-basis SP
6. ¹³C shifts ωB97X-D/6-31G(d) + empirical correction correct each conformer, then average 
%chk=nmr-hehre.chk
#p wB97XD/6-31G(d) NMR=GIAO

The ωB97X-V dual-basis step 5 is a Spartan method not available in Gaussian. This route line is only the shielding calculation of step 6. The correction δ_corr = Scale × δ_u + Σ[X0 + X1(R − L) + X2(R − L)²] and its coefficient tables are in SI Section C and must be applied to each conformer by hand (δ_u is the uncorrected shift).
