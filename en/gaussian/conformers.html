MM, HF and DFT

Molecular mechanics (MM) treats bond stretching and torsion with spring-like equations (a force field). It is fast because electrons are not computed, which makes it good for conformer searches. Bond formation/cleavage and electronic properties such as ECD are computed with quantum chemistry (QM).

Quantum chemistry (QM) obtains energies and properties from the electronic structure. Hartree–Fock (HF) is the most basic method, without electron correlation; DFT works from the electron density and approximates exchange–correlation with a functional. Routine organic calculations are almost all DFT.

In DFT you choose a functional (the exchange–correlation approximation) and a basis set (the functions representing the orbitals). Larger basis sets are more accurate, but the right combination depends on the functional and on the goal (geometry, NMR or excited states).

E, G and populations (concepts)

The electronic energy E (the SCF Done value) and the Gibbs free energy G (Sum of electronic and thermal Free Energies in the Freq output) are different quantities. G includes thermal motion and entropy, so conformer populations are usually computed from G (some protocols use E).

Populations follow the Boltzmann distribution pi ∝ exp(−ΔGi/RT), where ΔG is the difference between conformers computed under the same conditions. Mixing values from different functionals or basis sets gives meaningless populations.

To improve accuracy, run a single-point (SP) calculation with a larger basis set at the optimized geometry and use Ehigh + (Glow − Elow): the low-level thermal correction added to the high-level electronic energy.

Build the structure

Example: draw in ChemDraw (or the free ReShiki) and save as .mol → open in Avogadro, make it 3D, check stereochemistry (R/S), hydrogens, protonation state and charge, optimize lightly with MMFF94 → save as .mol2 → Spartan. Building directly in Spartan or Avogadro also works.

Conformer search

Search candidates with MM by varying rotatable bonds, ring shapes and OH orientations. In Spartan, choose Calculations → Conformer Distribution and the method (e.g. MMFF). CREST/xTB performs a similar search.

  • Use a generous energy window (e.g. 10–20 kcal/mol at MMFF). MM and DFT rank conformers differently.
  • Record the search settings and selection criteria.
  • When importing from other software, check atom order and stereochemistry after conversion.

Optimization and frequencies (Opt Freq)

Run Opt Freq on every candidate under the same conditions (functional, basis set, solvent). For each output, confirm convergence (Stationary point found) and zero imaginary frequencies.

  • If an imaginary frequency remains, look at its motion in a viewer such as GaussView, displace the structure slightly and reoptimize.
  • An optimization may slide into a different conformer. Check that the final structure is still the intended conformer.

Energy comparison and populations

Compute relative energies from the Gibbs free energy G and populations from the Boltzmann distribution. Use the experimental temperature (usually 298.15 K).

Removing duplicate conformers

When checking for duplicates, judge identity from heavy-atom overlay and O–H orientation, not energy alone, and record the keep/exclude decision. Keep the output files of excluded conformers as well.

Checking dihedral angles

Tabulating the key dihedral angles (signed, −180 to 180°) for each conformer makes it easy to spot duplicates and structures that moved to another conformer during optimization.

Population and weight equations

First confirm that every conformer is a minimum. Then confirm that each property calculation uses the same geometry as the one used for its population. Record failed and excluded conformers as well.

Populations used for averaging

Calculate populations p for the conformers left after removing duplicates, from the chosen energy and the temperature. X below is that energy per mole. Keep Gibbs free energies distinct from the electronic-energy-only approximation, and use units consistent with the gas constant R and temperature T.

pc = exp[−(Xc − Xmin)/(RT)] / ∑d exp[−(Xd − Xmin)/(RT)]

C = ∑c ∈ S pc, wc = pc/C, ∑c ∈ S wc = 1

S is the conformer set chosen in advance for the property calculation, and C is its total population (coverage); C = 1 when all conformers are included. Record the selected and excluded conformers and C. If a calculation in S fails, rerun it rather than renormalizing the weights over the remaining conformers. Average only after all results for S are in and the atom and conformer correspondence is checked.

Calculation conditions: Opt Freq

Use B3LYP/6-31G(d) for practice, or when an NMR or J protocol prescribes it for geometries; if a protocol prescribes another level, use that. When conformer energy differences matter, add a dispersion correction or single points with a larger basis set.

B3LYP/6-31G(d)

The basic choice for organic molecules. The Bally–Rablen J protocol assumes this gas-phase geometry.

Relative time: reference (1)

%chk=geom-b3.chk
#p B3LYP/6-31G(d) Opt=(Tight,MaxCycles=100) Freq

Used in: Bally & Rablen, J. Org. Chem. 2011, 76, 4818 · Zhang et al., J. Nat. Prod. 2025, 88, 2325

Source: Becke, J. Chem. Phys. 1993, 98, 5648

B3LYP/6-31+G(d,p)

Adds diffuse functions. Used as the geometry for PBE0 chemical-shift calculations (Li 2021, Grauso 2022).

Relative time: longer than 1 (larger basis)

%chk=geom-diffuse.chk
#p B3LYP/6-31+G(d,p) Opt=(Tight,MaxCycles=100) Freq

Used in: Li et al., Org. Lett. 2021, 23, 7134 · Grauso et al., J. Nat. Prod. 2022, 85, 2468

Source: Becke, J. Chem. Phys. 1993, 98, 5648

M06-2X/6-31++G(d,p), CPCM(H₂O)

A functional whose parameters cover medium-range dispersion. Used for polyphenol geometries and frequencies, without an added dispersion correction.

Relative time: longer than 1 (diffuse functions and solvent)

   SCRF=(CPCM,Solvent=Water) Integral=UltraFine SCF=Tight

Used in: Karwowski, Molecules 2026, 31, 1876

Source: Zhao & Truhlar, Theor. Chem. Acc. 2008, 120, 215

Combining SP with thermal corrections

Run the low-level Freq and the high-level SP at the same geometry and use G = Ehigh + (Glow − Elow), where Glow − Elow is the thermal correction printed by Gaussian. Add any standard-state conversion (e.g. 1 atm → 1 M) separately. If you apply a quasi-harmonic low-frequency correction (e.g. GoodVibes), avoid double counting.

Notes
  • Treat different protonation states or species as separate conformer sets.
  • Populations are computed only from the conformers the search found, so the completeness of the search determines the result.
