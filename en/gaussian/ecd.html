ECD: from TD-DFT to conformer-averaged curves

TD-DFT (e.g. TD=(NStates=30) on the route line) gives, for each excited state, the excitation energy, the oscillator strength (for UV) and the rotatory strength (for ECD). Each transition is broadened with a Gaussian, summed into a curve per conformer, and averaged by population for comparison with experiment.

  • The number of states (NStates) must cover the wavelength range you want to compare. Widening the plot does not add transitions that were not computed.
  • Rotatory strengths come in length and velocity forms. Record which one you used.
  • The band width and wavelength shift are display parameters. Keep them separate from the raw values.

The sign and shape of an ECD curve help decide absolute configuration, together with other evidence (NMR, optical rotation, X-ray).

Conditions for ECD/UV (TD-DFT)

Below the route line, continue with a blank line, title, blank line, charge/multiplicity, coordinates and a final blank line (input anatomy). Keyword meanings are in the keyword reference. Relative times are rough ratios to the setting marked “reference (1)”.

B3LYP/6-31G(d), IEFPCM(MeOH)

The basic setting for absolute-configuration work. 50 states is a generous example.

Relative time: reference (1, 50 states)

%chk=td-b3.chk
#p B3LYP/6-31G(d) TD=(Singlets,NStates=50)
   SCRF=(IEFPCM,Solvent=Methanol)

Used in: Zhang et al., J. Nat. Prod. 2025, 88, 2325

Source: Becke, J. Chem. Phys. 1993, 98, 5648 · Pescitelli & Bruhn, Chirality 2016, 28, 466

CAM-B3LYP/def2-SVP, SMD or IEFPCM(MeOH)

A long-range-corrected functional, more reliable than B3LYP when charge-transfer transitions are present. Reported with 30 states.

Relative time: similar to 1

#p CAM-B3LYP/Def2SVP TD=(NStates=30) SCRF=(SMD,Solvent=Methanol)

Source: Su et al., Nat. Prod. Bioprospect. 2020 · Xu et al., Nat. Prod. Bioprospect. 2019

ωB97X-D/6-31+G(d,p), PCM(CHCl₃)

Diffuse functions plus long-range correction. Li 2021 and Grauso 2022 used this functional and basis set for natural-product ECD; the input below is an example with chloroform as solvent and 50 excited states.

Relative time: longer than 1

%chk=td-wb.chk
#p wB97XD/6-31+G(d,p) TD=(Singlets,NStates=50)
   SCRF=(PCM,Solvent=Chloroform)

Used in: Li et al., Org. Lett. 2021, 23, 7134 · Grauso et al., J. Nat. Prod. 2022, 85, 2468

Source: Chai & Head-Gordon, Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 2008, 10, 6615 · Pescitelli & Bruhn, Chirality 2016, 28, 466

Building the curves

  • Broaden each transition with a Gaussian (typically about 0.3 eV FWHM; SpecDis's default σ = 0.16 eV is about 0.27 eV FWHM) and average the per-conformer curves by population.
  • A wavelength shift is sometimes applied when comparing with experiment (e.g. −7 nm in Xu 2019). Decide it per compound and record it.

Equations are in Curve equations below; SpecDis and similar programs can do this processing.

Optical rotation

B3LYP/aug-cc-pVDZ, IEFPCM(CHCl₃)

Optical rotation depends strongly on the basis set, so this example uses aug-cc-pVDZ, which includes diffuse functions. After the coordinate block: a blank line, then the wavelength line (e.g. 589nm), then a blank line.

Relative time: reference (1)

%chk=or-dz.chk
#p B3LYP/aug-cc-pVDZ Polar=OptRot CPHF=RdFreq
   SCRF=(IEFPCM,Solvent=Chloroform) Integral=UltraFine SCF=Tight

(title / charge mult / coordinates / blank line)
589nm

Used in: Yang et al., Molecules 2023, 28, 439

Source: Becke, J. Chem. Phys. 1993, 98, 5648 · Bursch et al., Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2022, 61, e202205735

  • Average the signed specific rotation at the same wavelength over conformers, and compare with experiment, stating the temperature, solvent and concentration in the same way.
  • When the value is small (tens or less) or the sign varies between conformers, assign the configuration together with ECD or NMR.

Curve equations (details)

Each transition i of each conformer c is broadened with a Gaussian at its excitation energy Eci, weighted by the population w_c, and summed.

K_ci(E) = exp[−((E − E_ci)/σ_E)²] / (σ_E √π)

Δε(E) = (1/22.96) Σ_c w_c Σ_i E_ci R_ci K_ci(E)      (ECD)
ε(E)  = 28700     Σ_c w_c Σ_i f_ci      K_ci(E)      (UV)

HWHH = σ_E √ln2,   FWHM = 2 HWHH,   E(eV) = 1239.84 / λ(nm)
  • E, Eci and σ_E in eV; R_ci is the rotatory strength from Gaussian output (10⁻⁴⁰ cgs); f_ci is the oscillator strength. Δε and ε in L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹.
  • σ_E is the half-width at 1/e of the maximum; e.g. σ_E = 0.4 eV corresponds to HWHH ≈ 0.333 eV. SpecDis starts at 0.16 eV; adjust for your molecule.
  • Broadening is done on the energy axis, then converted for wavelength display.
  • Building each conformer's curve under the same conditions and then averaging by population gives the same result because the operation is linear (so averaging GaussView curves in Excel is equivalent).

Comparing with experimental data

  • Put the experimental data in two columns, wavelength (nm) and intensity, with intensities converted to Δε or ε (L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹).
  • Convert data measured in mdeg or absorbance using the concentration and path length.
  • If you apply a wavelength shift or intensity scaling for the comparison, record the values and reasons separately from the raw calculated curve. Inverting the ECD sign gives the enantiomer's curve; do not use it to fit a curve of the same configuration to experiment.
  • Choose length or velocity rotatory strengths and use the same for all conformers. The UV curve uses the oscillator strengths f (length form).
