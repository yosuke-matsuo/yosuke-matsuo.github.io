Transition states: direct TS, QST2 and QST3

A transition state (TS) is the top of a reaction barrier: a maximum along the reaction direction and a minimum in every other direction (a first-order saddle point). Freq gives exactly one imaginary frequency whose motion corresponds to the bond changes of the reaction.

Method Structures needed Notes
Direct TS (Opt=(TS,CalcFC)) One guess close to the TS The most common method; success depends on a good guess.
QST2 Reactant and product Interpolates between the two structures. Atom ordering must match exactly.
QST3 Reactant, product and a TS guess QST2 stabilized with a TS guess.

With QST2/QST3, do not also write Opt=TS on the route line.

References

  • Gaussian: QST2/QST3
  • Peng, C.; Schlegel, H.B. Combining Synchronous Transit and Quasi-Newton Methods to Find Transition States. Isr. J. Chem. 1993, 33, 449–454.

Prepare a TS guess

First decide which bonds form or break. A common approach: set that bond distance to an intermediate value (e.g. 2.0–2.2 Å for C–C formation), freeze it, and relax everything else with Opt.

#p B3LYP/6-31G(d) Opt=ModRedundant

(title, blank, charge/mult, coordinates, blank line, then:)

1 12 F
  • Use the converged constrained geometry, with the constraint removed, as the starting point for the TS search.
  • Constrain only the bonds involved in the reaction; extra constraints distort the structure.

References

  • Avogadro: Constraints

TS optimization

#p B3LYP/6-31G(d) Opt=(TS,CalcFC) Freq

CalcFC computes the Hessian at the start. In a TS search the quality of the guess decides the outcome; when it fails, revising the guess is usually faster than changing settings.

For errors such as reaching the cycle limit or a wrong number of negative eigenvalues, see TS errors and TS re-search.

References

  • Gaussian: Opt

Verify with Freq

Run Freq at the converged TS under the same conditions and check two things.

  • There is exactly one imaginary frequency.
  • Play that mode in a viewer such as GaussView; its motion corresponds to the intended bond formation/cleavage.

If there are two or more, displace the structure along the extra mode and search again (see TS re-search).

References

  • Gaussian: Freq

IRC in both directions and endpoint check

%oldchk=ts_freq.chk
%chk=ts_irc_fwd.chk
#p B3LYP/6-31G(d) IRC=(RCFC,Forward,MaxPoints=60,StepSize=10) Geom=AllCheck Guess=Read

Run Reverse the same way. Look at the endpoint structures; if they are still changing, increase MaxPoints. Relax the endpoints with Opt Freq and confirm they fall into the reactant and product (or intermediate) minima.

Forward/Reverse are just labels; identify the reactant side from the structures.

Direct TS (Opt=TS)

The standard form when a good TS guess exists. CalcFC computes the initial Hessian. MaxStep=2 caps each step at 0.02 Bohr (default 0.30 Bohr): a cautious setting that needs more cycles.

Relative time: strongly guess-dependent

%chk=ts-direct.chk
#p M062X/6-31+G(d,p) Opt=(TS,CalcFC,Tight,MaxStep=2,MaxCycles=100)
   SCRF=(SMD,Solvent=Acetonitrile) Integral=UltraFine SCF=Tight

Source: Gaussian: Opt

Freq of the converged TS

Run Freq under the same conditions; confirm one imaginary frequency whose motion is the intended reaction.

Relative time: shorter than the TS search

%chk=ts-freq.chk
#p M062X/6-31+G(d,p) Freq
   SCRF=(SMD,Solvent=Acetonitrile) Integral=UltraFine SCF=Tight

Source: Gaussian: Freq

Conditions: IRC

HPC (default algorithm)

Start from the coordinates of the TS confirmed by Freq. Run Forward and Reverse as separate jobs. If the TS Freq .chk exists, RCFC with %oldchk reuses the Hessian instead of CalcFC.

Relative time: depends on path length and step size

%chk=irc-hpc.chk
#p M062X/6-31+G(d,p) IRC=(CalcFC,Forward,HPC,MaxPoints=80,StepSize=5)
   SCRF=(SMD,Solvent=Acetonitrile) Integral=UltraFine SCF=Tight

Source: Gaussian: IRC

LQA (when HPC fails in the corrector)

Same TS, direction and step size; only the algorithm changes to LQA.

Relative time: similar to HPC

%chk=irc-lqa.chk
#p M062X/6-31+G(d,p) IRC=(CalcFC,Forward,LQA,MaxPoints=80,StepSize=5)
   SCRF=(SMD,Solvent=Acetonitrile) Integral=UltraFine SCF=Tight

Source: Gaussian: IRC

Relax the IRC endpoints with Opt Freq and confirm they fall into the reactant and product minima. Compute the barrier from the G of TS and reactant under the same conditions and standard state.

TS re-search: overview

The procedure when a TS search stops with an eigenvalue error, or converges with two or more imaginary frequencies. The key idea: find the direction of the extra imaginary mode, displace slightly along it, and search again.

  1. Check the guess and the reaction direction

    Bond distances, atom orientation, clashes. Often fixable here.

  2. (If conditions allow) one short NoEigenTest probe

    Only 5 cycles.

  3. Mode-check Freq

    Run Freq at the stopped geometry to get complete vibrational modes.

  4. Pick the extra mode and displace ±

    Distinguish the reaction mode from the extra mode in a viewer such as GaussView and build two structures displaced ±0.05 Å along the extra one.

  5. Re-search the TS from both, then final Freq and IRC

    Search each with Opt=(TS,CalcFC); for the one that converges, run Freq and IRC in both directions.

Conditions for a NoEigenTest probe

Even when "Desired=1 Actual=2" appears, use it only when all of the following hold; then suspend the test for 5 cycles only:

  • A direct TS search (Opt=TS) using CalcFC.
  • The second negative eigenvalue has |value| ≤ 1×10⁻⁴ (optimizer units, not cm⁻¹).
  • The reaction geometry is intact at the stopped structure.
Opt=(TS,CalcFC,NoEigenTest,MaxStep=2,MaxCycles=5)

After 5 cycles, remove NoEigenTest and restore the test with a fresh CalcFC. If the extra negative curvature reappears, do not repeat NoEigenTest; proceed to the mode check.

Opt=(TS,CalcFC,MaxStep=2,MaxCycles=100)

Mode-check Freq

The eigenvectors printed by the Berny optimizer are partial, so they cannot be used as atomic displacements. Run Freq alone at the stopped geometry to obtain complete Cartesian modes.

#p B3LYP/6-31G(d) SCRF=(SMD,Solvent=Methanol) Freq

This Freq is for finding directions only; use the same functional, basis set and solvent as the TS search. Do not use it for TS proof, thermochemistry or starting an IRC.

Displacing ± along the extra mode

Play each imaginary mode in a viewer such as GaussView. The one matching the intended bond formation/cleavage is the reaction mode; any other is an extra mode. Do not decide by frequency magnitude or order.

  • Along the extra mode, build two structures with a maximum atomic displacement of 0.05 Å in the + and − directions (a guideline; range 0.01–0.20 Å).
  • The sign of a mode (+/−) is arbitrary, so try both.
  • Re-search each independently with Opt=(TS,CalcFC,MaxStep=2,MaxCycles=100).
  • If both fail, review the reason and the reaction geometry before increasing the displacement.

Final Freq and IRC after convergence

Run Freq at the converged stationary point under the same conditions; confirm exactly one imaginary frequency whose motion is the intended reaction. Then confirm the connection to reactant and product with IRC in both directions and endpoint Opt/Freq.

Record: the original TS, the probe, the mode-check Freq, the chosen mode, the displacement, the ± branches, and the final Freq/IRC.

Basis of the method

Displacing along an extra imaginary mode and re-searching is an established approach; the TURBOMOLE manual §5.2.4 describes a similar procedure with screwer. The 5-cycle probe, 0.05 Å initial displacement and 0.01–0.20 Å range are guideline values.

Alternative for a TS search that stopped early

When the first TS search hits a curvature error within a few cycles, a mode-check Freq at the stopped geometry is the first option and a fresh search from the original guess with MaxStep=2 is the second.

Notes
  • A converged constrained Opt or the top of a scan becomes a TS only after TS optimization and Freq confirm it.
  • A converged TS with one imaginary frequency is the TS of your reaction only once IRC confirms the connection to the intended reactant and product.
